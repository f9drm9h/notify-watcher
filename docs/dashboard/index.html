The encounter between these two vastly different worlds was catastrophic. Their ambition, fueled by Nixon's re-election campaign, would eventually lead them to the Democratic National Committee headquarters at the Watergate complex in the early hours of June 17, 1972, with wiretaps and cameras in hand, seeking to gain an illicit advantage over the opposition. What started as a "third-rate burglary" quickly spiraled into a constitutional crisis as the administration, from the highest levels, engaged in a systematic cover-up. Through hush money, obstruction of justice, and the destruction of evidence, Nixon and his inner circle desperately tried to distance themselves from the crime. Yet, the relentless reporting of journalists like Bob Woodward and Carl Bernstein, coupled with the principled stance of figures within the Justice Department and Congress, began to peel back the layers of deception. The existence of secret White House tapes, revealing Nixon's direct involvement in orchestrating the cover-up, ultimately proved to be the undoing of his presidency, leading to his resignation in August 1974, just before the House of Representatives could vote on articles of impeachment.
